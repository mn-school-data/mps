# How Minneapolis Public Schools is governed

Researched 2026-10-15. Sources are numbered in brackets and listed in [sources.md](sources.md). Items marked *(unverified)* come from search summaries or general Minnesota law and were not checked against primary text.

## 1. Legal status

- **What it is.** MPS is legally *Special School District No. 1, Minneapolis*, a "special independent school district" created by the Legislature in 1959 (Laws 1959, ch. 462), though the district had operated under other structures since 1860 [1][2]. Its charter is Minnesota Statutes **chapter 128D** ("1959 Basic Law, as amended") [2].
- **Powers.** Under 128D the board has all the powers of an ordinary independent school district board (Minn. Stat. ch. 123B) except where 128D, a special law, or a charter provision says otherwise [2]. In practice it runs on the same general school law as every other MN district, with a handful of Minneapolis-specific rules (board size and election districts, relationship to the city).
- **Independent of the city.** Boundaries are the same as the City of Minneapolis, but MPS is a separate unit of government from the City, Hennepin County and the Metropolitan Council, "with its own governing body, budgetary authority, and taxing power" [1]. 128D.03 says the district has no seat on the city's Board of Estimate and Taxation, while it does get a seat on the city planning commission under the city charter [2]. The city's practical role is running school board elections (Minneapolis Elections & Voter Services handles filing) [5].
- **State oversight.** MPS "is overseen by the Minnesota Department of Education" [1]. MDE controls the state funding formula and UFARS accounting rules, records the district's designated *business agent* (changed from the CFO to Ryan Strack in Jan 2026) [8], and enforces the *statutory operating debt* rule (Minn. Stat. 123B.81): if the general fund deficit passes 2.5% of operating expenditures the district must submit a state-approved plan to get out of it [9]. There is no state takeover mechanism in Minnesota law of the kind other states use *(unverified)*.
- **Proposed dissolution.** 2015 bills (HF 1995 / SF 1513) proposed dissolving SSD No. 1; they did not pass, and ch. 128D remains in force [2][3].

## 2. The Board of Education (who decides)

- **Composition.** Nine elected directors: six from geographic election districts and three at-large (citywide). The board went from 7 to 9 in 1987 (Laws 1987, ch. 218), and the 6-district/3-at-large split was set in 2006 [3][1]. Four-year staggered terms; about half the seats are elected each even year [1]. Two non-voting student representatives sit with the board [4].
- **2026 election.** Five seats (districts 1, 3, 5 and two at-large) are on the Nov 2026 ballot for terms Jan 2027–Jan 2031 [5]. That is a majority of the board.
- **Current members (Oct 2026)** [4]: Collin Beachy (Chair, at-large, to 2027), Kim Ellison (Vice Chair, at-large, 2029), Lori Norvell (Clerk, D5, 2027), Abdul Abdi (Treasurer, D1, 2027), Sharon El-Amin (D2, 2029), Lucie Skjefte (D3, 2027), Adriana Cerrillo (D4, 2029), Greta Callahan (D6, 2029), Joyner Emerick (at-large, 2027). Officers are chosen by the board each January [6].
- **What only the board can do** (per the ACFR, MPS web pages and general MN school law) [1][4][7]:
  - Hire, evaluate and fire the superintendent. Evaluation is done by the full board (Policy 2075) [4].
  - Adopt the annual budget by resolution; the board's appropriation is the district's "legal level of budgetary control" [1].
  - Certify the property tax levy: preliminary levy by Sept 30, final levy at the December Truth-in-Taxation meeting [10][4]. Operating and technology levy *increases* above formula need voter approval at referendum (e.g. the $20M tech levy approved Nov 2024) [10].
  - Approve collective bargaining agreements after union ratification (e.g. the Nov 2025 MFE contract, ~$35M general fund over two years) [11].
  - Adopt policy. Policies are "the laws that govern the school district" and are adopted by the board with the Policy Committee and superintendent (Policy 8520) [7].
  - Select the auditor and accept the annual audit (Bergan KDV for FY2025) [1].
  - Fill board vacancies by appointment until the next election (Policy 8116) [4][12].
- **Committees** prepare work for the full board but cannot act for it [4]:
  - *Finance*: chaired by the Treasurer (Abdi); members Ellison, Cerrillo, Emerick, Beachy. Staffed by the Senior Executive Officer (Strack). Charter: decide what budget-vs-actual information the board gets, set the budget decision calendar, oversee preparation of the annual budget and **quarterly** financial statements, and support staff on audit findings.
  - *Policy*: chaired by the Clerk (Norvell); staffed by General Counsel Jonassen and Strack (Policy 8120).
  - *Superintendent Evaluation*: designs the evaluation tools; the full board does the evaluation.
- **Meeting types** [4]: monthly regular business meetings (the only place routine votes happen; public comment), committee-of-the-whole / work sessions (discussion, no action), special meetings (called by Chair, Clerk or three directors), and closed executive sessions (allowed under the Open Meeting Law for things like labor strategy and personnel). Agendas and attachments are on BoardBook [13].

## 3. Executive structure (who runs it)

- **Superintendent** Dr. Lisa Sayles-Adams (since Feb 2024; previously Eastern Carver County). Described by MPS as "the leading decision-maker" who sets academic priorities [4]. By statute the superintendent is an ex officio non-voting member of the board, recommends hiring and dismissal of teachers, and supervises the schools; first contracts are capped at three years (Minn. Stat. 123B.143) [14]. The superintendent administers board policy and issues *regulations* (the administrative rules implementing policies) without a board vote [7].
- **Who reports to the superintendent** (org chart dated 6/30/2025, ACFR FY2025 p.11 [1]; titles updated from the MPS administration page, Oct 2026 [15]):

```
Board of Education
├── Superintendent (Sayles-Adams)
│   ├── Deputy Superintendent (Ty Thompson)
│   │     └── Associate Superintendents (3 supervise principals, ~20 schools each;
│   │         a 4th covers Special Ed & Student Support, listed on the 2026 page),
│   │         Communications, Partnerships, Equity/Climate, Student Support, Strategic Planning
│   ├── Senior Academic Officer (Melissa Sonnek; was Dr. Tia Clasen in 2025)
│   │     └── Community Ed, Special Ed, Early Childhood, Academics, Indian Ed,
│   │         Black / Latine Student Achievement, Multilingual & Magnet
│   ├── Senior Finance Officer (Ibrahima Diop in 2025; post vacant since Jan 2026, duties moved to Strack)
│   │     └── Executive Director, Finance → Controller
│   ├── Senior Human Resources Officer (Alicia Miller)
│   ├── Senior Operations Officer (Tom Parent): transportation, capital/facilities, risk, culinary, IT
│   └── Senior Executive Officer (Ryan Strack; in 2025 "Assistant to the Superintendent and Board")
│         └── Lobbyist / Government Relations, Family Ombudsperson, Staff Ombudsperson
└── General Counsel (Jamie Jonassen): reports to the superintendent, dotted line to the board
```

  Strack's office also had a dotted line to the board in 2025 (board relations). Principals report to associate superintendents, who report to the deputy superintendent [15].
- **Finance chain today.** After the Jan 2026 leave/removal of the Senior Finance Officer (Diop), Executive Director of Finance (Chapinduka, later terminated) and Controller (Gilbert), Strack, who had no prior finance-department role, became MDE business agent and took over finance duties, and the district hired an outside consulting firm (up to $830K) [8][16][17]. So the CFO function currently sits under a non-finance senior executive who also staffs both board committees.

## 4. What changed over the last ~5 years

- **Flatter top, fewer senior officers.** In June 2022 (Supt. Ed Graff) the chart had **eight** senior officers reporting to the superintendent (General Counsel; Academic; Accountability/Research/Equity; HR; Information; Communications/Engagement; Operations; Finance) plus four associate superintendents by school level [18]. By June 2025 that became a Deputy Superintendent, four senior officers (Academic, Finance, HR, Operations), the Assistant to the Superintendent and Board, and General Counsel, with research, communications, IT and associate superintendents consolidated under the Deputy or Operations [1]. Strack moved from "Administrator, Board & Government Relations" (2022) to "Assistant to Superintendent and Board" (2025) to "Senior Executive Officer" with finance authority (2026).
- **Finance leadership was unusually stable, then collapsed.** Diop was CFO from at least FY2016 to Jan 2026, with Chapinduka under him throughout [19][1]. A decade of audit findings on weak internal controls and segregation of duties [17] ended with the 2026 shakeup, IRS penalties of ~$5.3M [20], a ~$3M withheld health-trust payment [17], a separate $4.1M accounting error [21], and an FY2025 audit approved after the state deadline [17].
- **Turnover at the top.** Superintendents: Graff (2016–2022), interim Rochelle Cox (2022–24), Sayles-Adams (2024–). Board membership almost fully turned over between 2016 and 2025 (only Kim Ellison spans both) [19][1].

## 5. Where decisions actually happen, for the finance analysis

| Decision | Who proposes | Who decides | Public record |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual budget (June) and amendments | Superintendent / finance staff, via Finance Committee | Board resolution | BoardBook; budget book |
| Property tax levy | Staff | Board (Sept preliminary, Dec final); voters for referendum increases | TNT meeting, BoardBook |
| Labor contracts (largest cost driver) | Admin bargaining team; board briefed in closed session | Union ratifies, then board approves | Board agenda |
| School closures, program changes | Superintendent | Board | Board agenda |
| Staffing, school allocations, layoffs | Superintendent / HR / finance within budget | Administration | Budget book; layoff notices |
| Large contracts and purchases | Administration | Board approval above policy threshold (Policy 3300; state law requires bids over $175K, Minn. Stat. 123B.52) *(unverified threshold)* | Consent agenda |
| Accounting, payroll, tax filings | Finance office | Administration | Audit findings only |
