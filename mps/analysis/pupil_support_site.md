# Where the extra pupil support money went

Pupil support rose from $68.4M in FY2019, the last year before the pandemic, to $118.0M in FY2024, while enrollment fell 16%. It was $108.1M in FY2025 (audited). Labels below: *district* means the district's own board and budget documents (unaudited), *estimate* means our reading of how the pieces fit, and *press* means news reports not checked against district documents.

- **Nearly all of the rise is buses and vans.** Other student services (counselors, nurses, social workers, psychologists, attendance) stayed at about $35–40M. Transportation went from about $26M in FY2021, a distance-learning year, to an estimated $78–80M in FY2024. The district's own transportation budget was about $37M in FY2017–18, so it roughly doubled from before the pandemic.
- **Ridership did not grow.** About 19,400 students rode in spring 2024, including about 1,860 private school students (district). About a quarter of route spending is ordinary yellow bus service. About 70% goes to special education and to homeless, highly mobile and foster students, whom the law requires the district to carry, often door to door by van or taxi. That works out to under $1,000 a year per regular rider and about $14,000 per special education rider (estimate, FY2024 budget).
- **Each rider costs more than elsewhere in Minnesota.** Per rider, the FY2024 budget works out to about $3,200 overall against a state average of $1,203, about $950 per regular rider against $687, and about $14,000 per special education rider against $8,224 (estimate; state averages from the Minnesota Department of Education, 2022-23). The overall gap is mostly the mix, since about 3,000 door-to-door riders take 70% of route dollars, but prices within each group are also 1.4 to 2 times the state average.
- **Contract prices jumped about 50% in 2022.** During the bus driver shortage, the board added $16.5M of federal relief money to transportation, noting that contractor costs had "increased by nearly 50% since last year" (district, December 2022). Per-route prices have barely moved since; a regular bus route-day went from $495 to $507 between FY2023 and FY2026 (district contracts). Later growth came from more routes, not higher rates.
- **District drivers were replaced by contractors.** Transportation staff fell from 191 FTE in FY2016 to 155 in FY2024 and 113 in FY2025 (audited). The district now has more contracted providers than its own drivers.
- **Required door-to-door service grew.** About 1,900 special education students have home stops, and the number of homeless and highly mobile students rose 40% in one year (district). Their routes ($16.4M) now cost more than all regular routes combined.
- **Spending ran past contract caps and the budget.** Contract caps were raised repeatedly during the year. Pupil support came in $17.4M over budget in FY2024 (audited). The FY2025 audit found vendors paid more than their contracts allowed, and the district's corrective plan names transportation invoices. Press reports that one van contractor was paid about twice its cap have not been verified; vendor payment records compared with each contract's cap would confirm or rule this out.

**Who pays:** the state repays special education transportation at 100% of the prior year's cost through FY2025, and homeless student transportation since 2023. One year checks out to the dollar: FY2022 special transportation costs of $28.4M came back as FY2023 transportation aid (district). Costs coded wrongly do not count, and the district lost about $4M of aid on 2022-23 coding errors (press). The rate drops to 95% in FY2026 and 90% from FY2027. So perhaps $25–30M of the rise comes back as state aid a year later, and the net cost to the general fund grew by something like $20–25M (estimate).

**FY2027:** new three-year bus and van contracts fix prices for two years, then cap increases at inflation. Vendor caps total $52.2M against about $69M in FY2026 (district), but caps have been raised during the year in past years, so whether this becomes a saving is something to check against FY2027 actual vendor payments.
